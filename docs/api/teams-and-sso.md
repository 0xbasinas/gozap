# Teams, company sign-in and directory sync

Teams, single sign-on for companies (OpenID Connect), and directory sync (SCIM 2.0), including setup steps for Okta and Microsoft Entra ID.

## Teams

The Better Auth `organization` plugin is on (tables `organization`, `member`, `invitation`; `session.active_organization_id`). Anyone signed in can start up to 5 teams; a team holds up to 50 people; the starter is the `owner`. No sub-teams and no custom roles yet.

- `/teams` lists your teams (use one as the workspace, or open it). `/teams/<slug>` is the team's page: people, who joined and was invited recently, invite by email, cancel an invitation, remove a member, **make someone an owner** (an owner cannot leave while they are the only one), rename, and **delete after typing the team's name**. A team can have several owners.
- **Workspaces.** Inside the signed-in part of the site (`/app`, `/teams`, `/account`, `/admin`) a top bar has a workspace switcher: "Personal" (no active team) or one of the person's teams. Picking one calls `organization.setActive`, which stores `session.active_organization_id`; the Home page and the Teams page show which one is active. Public pages keep the plain site bar.
- **Scoping your own data to the workspace.** Call `workspaceOf(db, userId, sessionId)` (`apps/api/src/workspace.ts`) in any route that stores or reads workspace data; `GET /api/workspace` returns the same answer to the web app. It reads the active team from the database and counts it only while the person is still a member (nobody clears the active team of someone who was removed). Then limit every query to `teamId` (or, when it is null, to the person's own rows), give the table a foreign key to `organization` with `onDelete: 'cascade'`, and add it to the data export. The template's own example is the **to-do list** on the app home page (`apps/api/src/todos.ts`, table `todo`, `/api/todos`): Personal is your own list, a team's is shared by its members; any member can add and tick items, and only the author or a team owner/admin removes them. It is in the data export (`todos`), and deleting a team deletes its list.
- **This device remembers its workspace.** The switcher stores the last team (or Personal) in `localStorage` and returns to it after a new sign-in. It is only a convenience; the server still decides what the session may see.
- The invitation email (`teams.ts`) links to `/accept-invitation/<id>`, which asks the person to sign in with the invited address (`/sign-in?next=...`, a path on this site only) and then accept. Someone else's account cannot accept it. Invitations last 7 days, a re-invite cancels the old one, and an address that bounced gets nothing.
- All foreign keys cascade. Deleting an account also deletes teams nobody else belongs to (`soloTeamsDeletion`, same batch as the deletion); teams with other members stay. The data export lists a person's teams and their role.
- The audit trail records team created/deleted/left, invitations sent and accepted, and members removed.

## Single sign-on for companies (OpenID Connect)

`@better-auth/sso` is on, **OpenID Connect only**. Table `sso_provider` (cascades with the admin who added it, so deleting that admin removes the provider).

- **Who sets it up:** only an `admin` can register a provider (`providersLimit` is 25 for admins and 0 for everyone else), from the "Company sign-in" section of `/admin`: provider name, issuer URL, email domain, client id and secret. The secret is stored in the database row; treat the database as secret-bearing and use `defaultSSO` in code instead if you prefer to keep it out.
- **Proof of domain:** domain verification is on. A provider cannot sign anyone in until its domain has a DNS TXT record `_better-auth-token-<provider>.<domain>` with the token shown after registering; "Check the record" confirms it (a lookup that uses `node:dns/promises`, which works on Workers with `nodejs_compat`).
- **Signing in:** "Sign in with your company" on the sign-in page takes the work email, finds the provider by its domain and redirects to the company's login. An unverified domain answers 401. A new person is created on first sign-in; an existing person is linked only when the domain is verified.
- **A company can only create accounts at its own domain.** A `user.create.before` hook rejects a new account from `/sso/callback/<provider>` when the address is not at the provider's domain (or a subdomain). Without it a provider with a proven domain could create accounts, and keep a session, for any address. Tested.
- **SAML is refused** (`/sso/register` with a `samlConfig` answers 400). Checking a SAML response took about 10 to 15 ms in a local test, above the 10 ms CPU limit of the free Workers plan. Enable it only on the paid plan, after measuring in production.
- Tested here with a fake provider that signs real RS256 id tokens: a whole login (register, domain proof, authorize, callback, session), a person at another domain, and an unproven domain. A real login against Google Workspace, Okta or Entra has not been tried: test each in gozap before promising it to a customer.
- **Team-owned providers:** a team owner or admin can add up to 3 providers for their own team from the team page (the registration must name the team; without `organizationId` only staff may register, `providersLimit` is 25 for staff and 3 for others). A person who signs in through a team's provider joins that team as a plain `member` (`organizationProvisioning`); roles are never taken from the company's group claims. `sso_provider.organization_id` has no foreign key, so deleting a team (`/organization/delete`, by its owner) removes its providers and ends their sessions in a `before` hook.
- **Setup needs only the issuer:** the form calls `GET /api/sso-discover?issuer=` (staff, or an owner or admin of a team), which fetches the public discovery document (https, no redirects, 5 s, 100 KB, public host names only, the issuer inside must match) and returns the endpoints. They are handed to the plugin with `skipDiscovery`, so a customer's address never goes into the global `trustedOrigins`.
- **A team's own sign-in link:** `/sign-in?team=<slug>` signs in through the provider of that team (`organizationSlug`) without asking for an email.
- **Removing a provider** (`/sso/delete-provider`, by the registrant, staff, or the team's owner or admin) first ends the sessions of everyone linked to it; the plugin then deletes their linked accounts. Their user rows and team memberships stay. The audit trail records `sso_provider_added/updated/deleted` and `sso_user_provisioned`.
- **A page for each team:** `/t/<slug>/sign-in` shows "Sign in to <team name>" with one button that starts the team's company login (`organizationSlug`) and an email-link way out. `GET /api/team-sign-in/:slug` gives it the team name, only for a team with a company sign-in whose domain was proven; every other slug (unknown, no provider, unproven) answers the same 404, and the page then falls back to `/sign-in`. A signed-in visitor goes straight on.
- **Changing a provider that people already use.** The plugin refuses (409) to change the issuer, client id or any endpoint while linked accounts exist. We add the same for the domain (it is what sign-ins are matched on) and refuse `overrideUserInfo: true` (an identity provider rewriting names and addresses at each login). Changing the client secret and the scopes stays allowed; the new secret is encrypted like the first. A second administrator's approval was considered and not built: freezing is simpler and safer, and the way to move a provider is to delete it (which signs everybody who used it out; the confirm dialog now says so, and that it does not remove them from the team or from a directory) and add it again.
- **Identity provider addresses (`TRUSTED_IDP_ORIGINS` decision: not added).** Registration and updates check the issuer and every endpoint with the same rule as discovery (`publicHttps`): https only, a public host name, no IP address, no login in the address, no odd port; a plain `http://` or private address answers 400. Because the form hands the plugin the endpoints directly (`skipDiscovery`), the plugin's own discovery, and the `discovery_untrusted_origin` check that guards it, never runs, so no origin needs to be trusted. A company whose identity provider sits on a private network cannot be reached from a Worker anyway. If one day an exception is needed, add it as a deploy-time allow-list in `publicHttps`, not in `TRUSTED_ORIGINS` (which also decides which sites may call the API).
- **Private key instead of a shared secret (`private_key_jwt`).** Under "Advanced" in the form (and `tokenEndpointAuthentication: 'private_key_jwt'` on `/sso/register`), staff paste a PKCS#8 private key (`-----BEGIN PRIVATE KEY-----`), pick RS256, PS256 or ES256 and optionally a key id. Our server then signs a short statement (a client assertion) with it at the company's token endpoint, and the company checks it with the matching public key; no shared secret exists. The key travels in the `clientSecret` field, so the existing encryption at rest covers it (`secretStore.ts`; the stored row holds `enc:v1:...`, never the PEM). `src/privateKey.ts` refuses anything that is not a usable key for the chosen method (public keys, the wrong key type, junk, over 8000 characters) before it is stored, and `resolvePrivateKey` in `auth.ts` reads and decrypts it only while a person is signing in. Tested: a whole login against the fake provider, where the token request carries `client_assertion` and no `client_secret`. Not tried against a real provider.
- **Other form fields.** "Advanced" also takes extra permissions to ask for (`scopes`; openid, email and profile are always asked, names are checked to be plain tokens) and the names of the email and name fields in the company's tokens (`mapping.email`, `mapping.name`) for providers that use other names.
- **Login hint.** The address typed on the sign-in page, or a valid one in a team link (`/t/<slug>/sign-in?login_hint=ada@acme.com`), is passed as `loginHint`, so the company's login can fill it in. It is checked as an email address first.
- **Left out on purpose:** `additionalParams` (a free-form list added to the company's authorize address; nothing needs it, and it is a way to smuggle parameters), `mapping.extraFields` (nothing reads the extra fields) and code-defined `defaultSSO` providers (they have no database row, so no team ownership, domain proof, account export, audit events or removal flow; every company here is a row).

## Directory sync (SCIM 2.0) for teams

A company's directory (Okta, Microsoft Entra, JumpCloud ...) can add, update, switch off and remove its staff in a team. We built this ourselves because Better Auth's SCIM plugin needs database transactions, which D1 does not have; every multi-row write here is one `db.batch()`. Code: `src/scim.ts` (pure: tokens, filters, PATCH, resource shapes), `src/scimStore.ts` (database), `src/scimRoutes.ts` (routes). Tables: `scim_connection`, `scim_credential`, `scim_user`, `scim_group`, `scim_group_member` (all cascade with the team; `scim_user` also with the person, and is in their data export as `directory`).

- **Base URL:** `<site>/api/scim/v2`. Discovery (`ServiceProviderConfig`, `Schemas`, `ResourceTypes`) is public; everything else needs `Authorization: Bearer <token>`.
- **Tokens:** a team **owner** makes them on the team page ("Directory sync"): up to 3 connections per team, 5 live tokens each, expiry 1 to 365 days (default 90), shown once, stored only as an HMAC-SHA256 hash (key derived from `BETTER_AUTH_SECRET`; no new secret). Scopes `scim.users.read|write`, `scim.groups.read|write` (the page makes full-scope tokens; narrow one in the database if needed). "Last used" is written at most every 5 minutes. Revoking is immediate.
- **Users:** `GET` (list, by id), `POST`, `PUT`, `PATCH` (add, replace, remove; ordered and all-or-nothing; paths such as `name.givenName`, `emails[type eq "work"].value`, the enterprise extension, `manager` aliases), `DELETE`. `userName` is unique per connection ignoring case, `externalId` exactly. `active` also accepts `"True"`/`"False"` (Entra); `active: null` is 400. Not supported: passwords, `groups` on a user, photos, ims, x509, custom extensions.
- **Groups:** `GET`, `POST`, `PUT` (replaces the members), `PATCH` (`members` add, replace, remove, and `members[value eq "id"]`), `DELETE`. Members are direct users of the same connection (at most 1,000).
- **Filters:** `attribute eq "value"` joined by `and`, up to 10 (Users: `id`, `userName`, `externalId`, `emails.value`, `emails[type eq "work"].value`; Groups: `id`, `displayName`, `externalId`). `or`, `not`, `pr`, comparisons and parentheses answer `invalidFilter`. Paging: `startIndex` (from 1), `count` (default and most 100). `attributes` / `excludedAttributes` work on top-level names. No bulk, sort, ETags or `/Me`.
- **What provisioning does:** `POST /Users` creates a sign-in account (email not yet verified; the person proves it by signing in) and adds them to the team as a `member`. An existing account is adopted only when its address is at a domain this team has proven with a DNS check on a company sign-in provider; otherwise 409. Email is never used to link otherwise. The same `externalId` coming back after a deactivation reuses the old record.
- **What switching off does:** the person leaves the team (an owner is never removed). If the directory created the account, the person has no other team and is not staff, the account is also suspended (`banReason: deprovisioned`) and signed out; switching them back on lifts it. A switched-off person cannot be put back in the team by an invitation or by company sign-in: every `member` row goes through the database adapter, which refuses it (`secretStore.ts`).
- **Deleting** a SCIM user, a group or the whole connection removes only directory records; accounts and memberships stay.
- **Groups can make team admins, only if an owner says so.** A group does nothing by default. A team owner ticks a group on the team page ("Directory sync"); everyone in it is then an admin, and loses that again on leaving the group, being switched off, or the group being removed or unticked. Only rights the group gave are taken back, owners are never touched, and nothing here can make an owner. Anyone who manages that group in the directory can now decide who administers the team, which is why the owner has to opt in and is warned.
- **Tombstones.** When a person deletes their account (or staff remove it) while the directory still lists them, a salted hash of their address is kept for 90 days (`scim_tombstone`; not the address itself). The next sync's create is answered with 409 instead of silently recreating the account. The owner sees "N deleted accounts are not being recreated" and can choose Allow again. A person who has an account again (they signed in) is not blocked.
- **Setting up Okta or Entra:** SCIM base URL `<site>/api/scim/v2`, authentication "HTTP header" / bearer with the token, unique identifier `userName` (the work email), push new users, profile updates and deactivation. Send the sign-in subject (OIDC `sub`) as `externalId`.
- **Company sign-in only links to a person the directory has not switched off.** When the identity provider's login would be attached to an account (`ssoLinkRefusal` in `scimStore.ts`, called from the database adapter in `auth.ts` because the plugin's own transaction-based check cannot run on D1), it is refused if the team's directory lists that person as inactive, or if the identity's subject is the directory's `externalId` of a different person (a matching email alone never attaches an identity to someone else's account). Switching a person off also deletes the company sign-in link on their account in the same batch (owners excepted), so the identity provider cannot sign them back in as that account; switching them on lets the next login link again. Teams without a directory, and people the directory does not list, are not affected. A person the directory created has an unverified address, and the plugin links only verified addresses, so their first company login works after they have proven the address once (a magic link).

### Setting up Okta

1. On the team page, **Directory sync > New connection**; copy the token (shown once).
2. In Okta, open the OIDC app for this team > **General > App settings** and, in the app's **Provisioning** tab, **Configure API integration**. Choose the SCIM 2.0 template ("SCIM 2.0 Test App (Header Auth)" or your own app with SCIM enabled).
3. SCIM connector base URL `https://<site>/api/scim/v2`; unique identifier field `userName`; supported actions: Push New Users, Push Profile Updates, Push Groups (optional); authentication mode HTTP Header, with the token as the **Authorization** value (`Bearer <token>`). Use **Test Connector Configuration**.
4. In **To App**, enable Create Users, Update User Attributes and Deactivate Users. Set the `userName` mapping to the work email address and `externalId` to the same value as the OIDC `sub` (Okta user id, `user.id`), because that is how a login is tied to a person.
5. Assign people (or a group) to the app. They appear under Directory sync on the team page; deactivating or unassigning them in Okta switches them off here.

### Setting up Microsoft Entra ID

1. On the team page create a connection and copy the token.
2. Entra admin center > **Enterprise applications > New application > Create your own application** (non-gallery). Open **Provisioning**, set mode **Automatic**.
3. **Tenant URL** `https://<site>/api/scim/v2`; **Secret token** the token. **Test connection** (it sends a lookup by `userName`; unknown users are answered with an empty list, which Entra expects).
4. **Mappings > Provision Microsoft Entra ID Users**: keep `userPrincipalName` > `userName` (or map `mail`, whichever is the work email), `objectId` > `externalId` (the same value as the OIDC `oid`/`sub` your sign-in sends), and `Switch([IsSoftDeleted], , "False", "True", "True", "False")` > `active`. Remove attributes we do not store if the log warns about them. Groups are optional.
5. Set **Provisioning Status** on and the scope to "Sync only assigned users and groups". Entra syncs about every 40 minutes; **Provision on demand** is the quick way to try one person.
6. Entra sends `active` as the strings `"True"`/`"False"`; both are accepted. If the validator reports a failure on a feature listed as unsupported above (bulk, sort, ETags), it is expected, not a defect.

Both vendors' steps are described from their documentation; they have not been run against real tenants yet (see "Not yet tried against a real directory" below).

- Tested with `src/scim.test.ts` (filters, PATCH, groups) and the "directory sync" block in `test/api.test.ts` (tokens, discovery, expiry and rotation, provisioning, adoption rule, off and on, owner kept, PUT, return of a person, groups, isolation between teams, scopes, rejoin refusal, export, removal). Not yet tried against a real directory: run the Okta or Entra provisioning test against a preview copy first.
